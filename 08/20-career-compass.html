01 A Framework For Teenagers & Parents

The wrong question
every teenager is asked

The biggest mistake is trying to answer "What profession should I choose?" while still in school — before there's any evidence to answer it with.

02 A Better Question

"What evidence do I have that I should spend 10–15 years becoming a doctor, lawyer, engineer or CA — rather than becoming exceptionally good at creating and selling things?"

You can actually build a decision system around this question — instead of guessing at fifteen.

03 Step 1 — Don't Decide From Grades

School marks are a weak predictor of career fit.

Instead, observe what kind of work actually gives you energy:

04 Step 2 — Run Experiments, Not a Life Decision

A 15-year-old doesn't need to decide. They need to try things.

05 Step 3 — The Real Discovery

It's not your career.
It's your comparative advantage.

The last experiment — selling something real — teaches what school almost never does: does this person actually enjoy creating economic value?

07 Step 4 — The Opposite Mistake Is Also a Mistake

"Degrees are useless, start a business" is bad advice too.

Medicine, law, engineering and CA are credential-heavy professions with barriers to entry for a reason. If you discover at 16 that you genuinely want to be a doctor — don't waste three years on dropshipping because a YouTuber said entrepreneurship is the future.

The real question: does this career require the degree, and do I actually want the underlying work?

08 Step 5 — One Almost-Universal Skill

Learn sales — regardless of the career.

Even a future doctor needs to communicate with patients. A lawyer needs persuasion. An engineer needs to sell ideas internally. A CA needs clients. An entrepreneur needs customers.

09 Step 6 — A Career Laboratory (Ages 13–18)

Don't force the decision. Escalate the experiments.

13–14

Explore everything: coding, debating, selling, writing, science, video, design, sport, teaching.

15–16

Start building: a website, a YouTube channel, a small product, tutoring, a school club, a simple app.

16–17

Start making money — even ₹500 matters, then ₹5,000, then ₹50,000. Can you spot a problem and get someone to pay for the fix?

17–18

Now make the serious decision — backed by three years of actual evidence, not self-perception.

11 Closing Thought

The goal isn't to eliminate degrees. It's to stop students from spending 5–10 years collecting credentials before discovering what they actually enjoy doing.
