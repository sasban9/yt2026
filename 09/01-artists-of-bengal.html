An Archive in the Making · West Bengal

Bengal's masters are making history. We're making sure none of it is lost.

ARTISTS of Bengal is a living archive of the state's exceptional living artists and artisans — recorded not once, but again and again, so the world can see not just what they make, but how they, and their craft, change over time.

The Gap

Bengal doesn't lack extraordinary makers. It lacks a record that keeps up with them.

01

Documented once, then forgotten

A ten-year-old video or a single photo shoot becomes the only record of a career that kept evolving long after the camera left.

02

Craft changes faster than the archive

Techniques shift, apprentices take over, materials change — but most documentation captures one moment and calls it complete.

03

Visibility isn't the same as vitality

An artisan can be "findable" online and still be practising a very different craft, in very different circumstances, today.

Our Approach

We don't take one photograph and call it done. We return.

Most craft documentation is a single visit — a portrait, a paragraph, a price. We treat that as a starting point, not an ending. Every master we archive is revisited over time, so the record grows the way the person and their practice actually do.

Portrait & process, recorded again each visit

Face, hands, studio, tools, technique — captured on a returning basis, not as a one-time shoot, so change is visible rather than assumed.

Voice, preserved in the original Bengali

Interviews are recorded primarily in Bengali and kept as the archival source of truth — translated for the world, never replaced by the translation.

Provenance, dated and verified

Every award, exhibition, and documented work is logged with a date and a source — so the record can be trusted, not just admired.

How An Archive Grows

Three steps, repeated for as long as the master keeps working.

Discover

We identify a master through recognition, craft tradition, and word of mouth from researchers, institutions, and the community around them.

Document

A field visit records portraits, process, ambient sound, and an unhurried interview — ideally while the person is working, not performing.

Return

We come back. New work, new circumstances, new chapters get added to the same record, instead of starting a new one each time.

Editorial Principles

Rules we hold ourselves to

—

Master first, circumstances second. Hardship is part of some stories, but it is never the hook. We lead with the work.

—

Verified, not vague. Every award and claim carries a year, an issuing body, and — where possible — a source. No unearned superlatives.

—

Recognition is earned, not purchased. Inclusion in the archive is editorial. It is never for sale.

—

Privacy is a feature. A master doesn't need to become a performer or an influencer to be represented well.

Help us keep the record honest — and growing.

Whether you're a researcher, a collector, an institution, or simply someone who knows a master we should meet, the archive gets stronger with every introduction.
