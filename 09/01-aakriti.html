SUBIR BISWAS

Swapno Udaan

The exploration of metal in sculptural form has been one of the preoccupations of artist Janardhanan Rudhramoorthy, over the last decade. Deeply inspired by nature, he has found various means to adapt the hard and heavy medium into visibly fluid and dynamic objects. Coming from a background in painting, Jana was drawn to processes of three-dimensionality that translated and presented his concepts in ways beyond the surface of a canvas. This led to interesting experiments early in his career, including composite forms like the creation of a beehive with nails on wood, and meticulously shaped wire arrangements representing human anatomy.

As he expertly pursues potential shapes and figures, Jana’s grammar has grown to accommodate visual and material paradoxes. Solid iron is teased into softness, furthering the experience of malleable matter that can bend and turn with ease.

Dense sheets of metal are forged into multiple slender strips, developing the illusion of transparency and graceful movement. Despite the large scale, many forms are sculpted and displayed to emulate lightness and the lack of gravity, defying the nature of the material.
